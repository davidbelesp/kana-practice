# Build the beat before chasing speed

Japanese timing is organized around morae. Paying attention to each beat prevents several sounds that look small on the page from disappearing in speech.

## Small っ: hold the next consonant

Small っ takes one beat and delays the consonant that follows: きて is **ki-te**, while きって is **ki-(hold)-te**. It does not add a separate *tsu* sound.

## Long vowels

Vowel length can distinguish words. In hiragana, an added vowel often extends the previous one: おばさん and おばあさん do not sound identical. An o sound is frequently lengthened with う, as in こう. Count the extra vowel as a mora.

## The moraic nasal ん

ん owns a beat. Its exact sound adapts to what follows, so it can resemble **m** before b or p, but its rhythmic place remains.

## Natural speech

The vowels i and u can become very light between voiceless consonants or at the end of some expressions. This is **devoicing**, not deletion. Japanese also uses patterns of relatively high and low pitch. Notice pitch from recordings, but prioritize clear mora timing before trying to imitate every pattern.
