# Small expressions, real social work

Japanese greetings are short social actions. The best expression depends on time, relationship, and what is happening—not only on a dictionary translation.

## 20 common expressions

## Meeting and parting

- **おはようございます** is a polite morning greeting; **こんにちは** suits much of the daytime; **こんばんは** is used in the evening.
- **はじめまして** opens a first meeting. A self-introduction often closes with **よろしくおねがいします**, which politely asks for a good relationship.
- **おやすみなさい** is said before sleep. **さようなら** can sound more final than casual English “bye,” so everyday departures use several other expressions.

## Thanks, apologies, and attention

**ありがとうございます** gives polite thanks. **すみません** can apologize, attract attention, or acknowledge that someone has gone to trouble for you. Context decides which shade is strongest.

## Home and meals

The person leaving home says **いってきます**; someone staying replies **いってらっしゃい**. On returning, **ただいま** receives **おかえりなさい**. Before a meal say **いただきます**; after it say **ごちそうさまでした**.

Practice by imagining a specific moment and choosing the expression that performs the right social action.
