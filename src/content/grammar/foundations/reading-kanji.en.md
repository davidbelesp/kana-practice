# Read kanji through words

Kanji are characters used to write many core meanings. A character is not simply a picture or a single sound: it participates in words, and the word tells you which reading is active.

## Meaning and reading

山 carries the idea “mountain,” but reading always happens in context. **On readings** entered Japanese through historical contact with Chinese; **kun readings** connect a character's meaning with native Japanese words. These labels explain origins, not a rule that predicts every word.

## Learn vocabulary, not isolated sound lists

When a character has several readings, memorize useful words containing it. The complete word gives pronunciation, meaning, and usage together. Two or more kanji can form a compound: 火 “fire” plus 山 “mountain” forms 火山 “volcano.”

## Components and construction

Characters contain recurring components. Some hint at a meaning family, others sometimes suggest a sound, and some mainly distinguish the written form. Treat them as clues rather than guarantees. Consistent stroke order supports balanced handwriting and makes unfamiliar forms easier to analyze.

A strong beginner goal is modest: recognize frequent characters inside real words, write a small core clearly, and keep adding vocabulary.
