# Hiragana as a sound map

Hiragana is a syllabary: most symbols represent one **mora**, a steady rhythmic unit. Start with the five vowels あ・い・う・え・お, then notice how consonant rows reuse that vowel order.

## Read the shape as a sound

Some standard romanizations are not mechanically predictable: し is **shi**, ち is **chi**, つ is **tsu**, and ふ is **fu**. Treat the kana itself as the target; rōmaji is only a label.

## Marks that change a row

The two-stroke **dakuten** mark voices a consonant: か → が, さ → ざ, た → だ, and は → ば. The small circle **handakuten** changes the h-row to p: は → ぱ.

## Small や・ゆ・よ

After an i-row kana, small ゃ・ゅ・ょ creates a contracted sound: きゃ is **kya**, not *kiya*. The pair still occupies one mora. Compare りゅ (**ryu**) with りゆ (**ri-yu**).
