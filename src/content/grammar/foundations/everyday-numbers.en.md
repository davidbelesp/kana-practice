# Assemble numbers instead of memorizing a list

Japanese numbers become predictable once you know the digits and the multiplier じゅう “ten.” Learn sound patterns rather than memorizing every value separately.

## Core readings

0 is れい or ゼロ. The basic sequence is いち, に, さん, よん, ご, ろく, なな, はち, きゅう, じゅう. Four, seven, and nine also have alternate readings, but よん, なな, and きゅう are reliable beginner choices in simple counting.

## Build the tens

Place a digit before じゅう to make a multiple of ten, then add a digit after it:

- 20: にじゅう
- 36: さんじゅうろく
- 74: ななじゅうよん
- 99: きゅうじゅうきゅう

One hundred is **ひゃく**, not *じゅうじゅう*. Numbers later change pronunciation with some counters, but first make the plain 0–100 pattern automatic.

## Two-way practice

Train both directions: see 58 and say ごじゅうはち; hear or read ろくじゅうさん and produce 63. This prevents recognition from becoming stronger than recall.
