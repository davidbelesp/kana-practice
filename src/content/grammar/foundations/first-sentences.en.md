# Build a sentence around information

Think of a Japanese sentence as a small information scene. One expression establishes the frame, another supplies the news, and short markers reveal how those pieces relate. Here we will test that model with **は**, **が**, **の**, **か**, and the polite ending **です**.

> **Important:** the topic particle is written **は** but pronounced **wa**. Write わ only when it is actually the hiragana syllable *wa*, as in わたし.

## A visual key for information roles

- <span class="grammar-key grammar-topic">Blue</span> — the topic, subject, or noun phrase being anchored
- <span class="grammar-key grammar-particle">Purple</span> — a grammatical particle
- <span class="grammar-key grammar-about">Green</span> — the information, description, or related noun
- <span class="grammar-key grammar-verb">Red</span> — **です**, the polite copula or sentence ending

The colors show each word's job. They are learning aids, not part of Japanese writing.

## Set the frame with は

**は** places a frame around the conversation: “For this topic, here is the information.” English normally leaves that frame implicit, so **わたしはレイラです** becomes the natural “I am Leila.”

Use **は** when:

- introducing a person or thing as the topic;
- continuing with a topic already known from context;
- making a general statement;
- contrasting one topic with another.

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">わたし</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">レイラ</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<br><small>I am Leila.</small></div>
The speaker is the frame for the new information.

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">にほんご</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">おもしろい</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<br><small>Japanese is interesting.</small></div>

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">わたし</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">がくせい</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<span class="grammar-topic">あね</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">せんせい</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<br><small>I am a student. My older sister, by contrast, is a teacher.</small></div>

**Common mistake:** do not write the particle as わ. Its pronunciation changes, but its spelling stays **は**.

## Point to the subject with が

**が** marks the grammatical subject: the person or thing that performs an action, exists, or has a quality. It often presents **new, unknown, or specifically requested information**.

Use **が** when:

- answering “who?” or “what?”;
- marking a question word such as だれ or なに as the subject;
- reporting that someone arrived or that something exists;
- focusing on exactly which person or thing has a quality.

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">だれ</span><span class="grammar-particle">が</span><span class="grammar-about">せんせい</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span><span class="grammar-particle">か</span>。<br><small>Who is the teacher?</small></div>

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">くぼさん</span><span class="grammar-particle">が</span><span class="grammar-about">きました</span>。<br><small>Kubo has arrived. Kubo is the newly identified participant.</small></div>

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-about">へやに</span><span class="grammar-topic">つくえ</span><span class="grammar-particle">が</span><span class="grammar-about">あります</span>。<br><small>There is a desk in the room.</small></div>

### は or が?

Compare the speaker's intention:

- **くぼさんはせんせいです。** — Kubo is already our frame; the sentence adds information about Kubo.
- **くぼさんがせんせいです。** — Kubo is the person being identified as the teacher.

Neither particle simply means “is.” Context decides whether you are setting a topic with **は** or identifying a subject with **が**.

## Build a noun relationship with の

**の** links one noun to another. The noun before **の** describes the noun after it. Depending on context, English may use an apostrophe, “of,” or a compound noun.

Use **の** for:

- possession: whose object it is;
- affiliation: a school, company, or group;
- category or subject: what kind of thing it is;
- personal relationships.

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">わたし</span><span class="grammar-particle">の</span><span class="grammar-about">ほん</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<br><small>It is my book.</small></div>

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">にほんご</span><span class="grammar-particle">の</span><span class="grammar-about">ほん</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<br><small>It is a Japanese-language book.</small></div>

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">ともだち</span><span class="grammar-particle">の</span><span class="grammar-about">おかあさん</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">いしゃ</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<br><small>My friend's mother is a doctor.</small></div>

The order matters: **レイラさんのノート** is “Leila's notebook.” For a longer chain, find the final noun first: in **あにのバンドのしゃしん**, しゃしん is the main noun, バンド describes the photo, and あに tells us whose band it is.

## Open a question with final か

Place **か** at the end of a complete polite sentence to turn it into a question. You do not need to change the word order.

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">くぼさん</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">せんせい</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span><span class="grammar-particle">か</span>。<br><small>Is Kubo a teacher?</small></div>

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">これ</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">なん</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span><span class="grammar-particle">か</span>。<br><small>What is this?</small></div>

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">レイラさん</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">きょう きます</span><span class="grammar-particle">か</span>。<br><small>Will Leila come today?</small></div>

After a noun or description, the polite pattern is **ですか**. After a polite verb such as **きます**, add **か** directly: **きますか**, never **きますですか**.

## Choose a polite landing with です

**です** is not a particle and is not a universal replacement for the verb “to be.” It is a polite copula or sentence ending used after nouns and な-adjectives. It can also add politeness after an い-adjective.

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">これ</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">えんぴつ</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<br><small>This is a pencil.</small></div>

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">くぼさん</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">げんき</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<br><small>Kubo is well.</small></div>

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">このほん</span><span class="grammar-particle">は</span><span class="grammar-about">おもしろい</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span>。<br><small>This book is interesting.</small></div>

Do not put **です** after a verb that is already polite:

- Correct: **レイラさんはきます。** — Leila will come.
- Incorrect: **レイラさんはきますです。**

## Read the layers from the outside in

These pieces can work together:

<div class="grammar-example"><span class="grammar-topic">これは</span><span class="grammar-about">レイラさん</span><span class="grammar-particle">の</span><span class="grammar-about">にほんごのノート</span><span class="grammar-verb">です</span><span class="grammar-particle">か</span>。<br><small>Is this Leila's Japanese notebook?</small></div>

Read it in layers: **レイラさんの** establishes the owner, **にほんごの** narrows the kind of notebook, **です** gives the statement a polite landing, and **か** asks the listener to confirm it.
